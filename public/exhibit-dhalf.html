Two days into the investigation, Thahani calls you in early. Her face looks different this time — tighter, more serious.

THAHANI · DIRECTOR OF SECURITY "Inchaf didn't come to work this morning," she says. "Building security checked on him. He's dead. He was found in his flat last night. Police are calling it an accident for now, but the timing —" She doesn't finish the sentence. She doesn't need to.

This case just changed. It isn't only about stolen software anymore.

If Ghost_Fen used Inchaf to get access — a password, a badge, someone looking the other way at the right moment — and Inchaf became a problem once the breach was found, then "accident" is a word Thahani isn't ready to believe. She can't get the police to reopen the case on a feeling alone. She needs a timeline. A clear, minute-by-minute record of where Ghost_Fen was online around the time Inchaf died.

That timeline is sitting in the evidence you still haven't finished checking.

THAHANI · DIRECTOR OF SECURITY "Finish the logs," Thahani says. "Finish the laptop. I need to know if he was online when Inchaf died. And if he was, I need proof strong enough for a detective, not just a security report."
